University Student Productivity Platform

Feature Specification

Working title: UniHub
Purpose: A centralized workspace that helps university students organize their academic life, manage learning materials, work with documents, prepare assignments and theses, and keep track of deadlines.

1. Product Vision

The application provides a single place for students to manage their entire university workflow. Instead of switching between calendars, document folders, task managers, note-taking applications, and AI tools, students can connect all their academic information in one system.

The application should not merely store information. It should establish connections between modules, lectures, documents, assignments, deadlines, and research projects.

For example, a lecture can contain its notes and presentation, link to a module, and generate tasks for an upcoming exam. A thesis project can contain research papers, annotations, references, writing progress, and milestones.

Core principles

Centralization: One place for all university-related information.

Contextual organization: Documents, tasks, appointments, and notes are linked to their relevant modules.

Productivity: Reduce administrative work and make deadlines visible.

AI assistance: Automate repetitive work such as summarization, document analysis, and research organization.

Student-focused design: Simple to use, flexible across different degree programs, and suitable for both everyday studying and major academic projects.

Data ownership: Students retain control over their documents, notes, and personal academic information.

Extract definitions, formulas, dates, and important claims.

Identify key people, theories, and concepts.

Extract assignment requirements and deadlines from documents.

Identify references and bibliographic information.

Learning assistance

Generate flashcards and practice questions.

Clearly distinguish source content from AI-generated interpretations.

Avoid presenting uncertain AI-generated information as fact.

Preserve the original document.

17. Success Criteria

The product should be evaluated against practical outcomes rather than the number of features implemented.

Students can find any module, task, or document quickly.

Every module has a clear overview of its associated materials and deadlines.

Tasks and deadlines can be managed without duplicating information.

Students can annotate and summarize documents in one workflow.

Research sources remain connected to their notes and thesis sections.

AI-generated summaries and answers are traceable to source material.

Students can export their work without being locked into the application.

The interface remains usable even when a student has multiple semesters and hundreds of documents.

Conclusion

The central idea is to create a connected academic workspace rather than another standalone task manager or note-taking app.

The most important architectural decision is to link the core entities—modules, events, documents, notes, tasks, and projects—so that information created in one area is immediately useful in the others. AI, thesis support, and exam preparation can then build on that shared foundation.
